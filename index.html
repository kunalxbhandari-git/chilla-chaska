Why a chilla

A breakfast that is already good for you — before anyone markets it that way.

Chilla is not a health-food invention. It is a lentil pancake that half of north India already eats. Our job is not to explain the category. It is to cook it properly, every single time.

Protein you can point at

Gram flour and lentils are the batter, not an additive. Add paneer or sprouts and one chilla clears 30 g without a scoop of anything.

11 g – 32 g per chilla

No maida, no colour

Nothing refined, nothing dyed. The yellow is turmeric. The green flecks are coriander that was whole ten minutes ago.

Besan & dal chillas are gluten-free

Cooked when you order

No warming tray, no batch made at 7 a.m. and sold at noon. Batter goes on the tawa after the ticket prints.

6 – 10 min per chilla

Recipe cards, not guesswork

Every chilla has a written spec — batter ratio, gram weights, spread diameter. The cook changes; the chilla does not.

Portion-controlled to the gram

Anatomy

Look inside a chilla.

Pick one. The plate redraws, and the numbered pins walk you through what is actually happening on the tawa — batter, fold, flecks and all.

Lace edge

Tap a numbered pin on the chilla to read what is going on at that spot.

Besan Chilla

₹69

Stone-ground gram flour, carom seed and a fistful of coriander, spread thin on a 210 °C tawa until the rim turns to lace.

Cross-section

Five layers, bottom to top.

Tawa face

The side that touched the iron. Matte, toasted, structurally the floor of the whole thing — and the side we always lay down in the box.

Set batter

Besan or ground dal, thickened by heat rather than by flour. This is the protein layer and it is 2–3 mm, no more.

Fleck line

Onion, tomato, chilli and coriander, folded in cold so they cook for seconds, not minutes. This is where the chilla gets its smell.

Stuffing

Paneer, cheese, corn or sprouts, laid across the centre and sealed under the fold while the base is still tacky.

Chutney & curd

Never poured on. Coriander-mint chutney and set curd travel in their own sealed pots so the lace edge arrives dry.

Kitchen sequence

Six steps, and the order is not negotiable.

This is a real sequence, not a graphic. Skip the rest, and the batter tears when you spread it. Skip the vent, and a crisp chilla arrives limp.

STEP 01

Soak

Moong and chana go into water the previous evening. Four hours minimum; overnight for the dal chillas.

4–10 hrs · previous day

STEP 02

Wet-grind

Ground with ginger, green chilli and jeera at 6 a.m. Never dal flour. You can taste the difference in one bite.

6:00 AM · daily

STEP 03

Rest the batter

Twenty minutes, covered. It hydrates and relaxes — this is what lets you spread it paper-thin without a tear.

20 min

STEP 04

Hot tawa, thin spread

Iron tawa held around 210 °C. One ladle, one spiral from the centre out. The rim crisps before the middle sets.

2–3 min, first side

STEP 05

Stuff & fold

Stuffing goes across the centre while the base is still tacky, then the fold is pressed for five seconds so it welds.

2–4 min

STEP 06

Vent & go

Into a vented kraft box, tawa-face down, chutney sealed separately. Steam is the enemy of a crisp edge.

< 60 sec to handover

Delivery

A crisp chilla that survives the ride.

The honest risk with this category is that a chilla loses its edge in a closed box. So the packaging is part of the recipe, and we test it the same way we test the food.

Vented kraft box

Perforated lid. Trapped steam is what turns a lace edge into a napkin — the vents let it out on the way to you.

Chutney rides separately

A sealed 30 ml pot, never poured over the chilla. Same for curd. You assemble it; we just get it there dry.

Bagasse and kraft only

Sugarcane-pulp bowls, kraft bags, wooden cutlery. No plastic clamshells, which sweat and ruin the texture anyway.

3–5 km radius

We cap the delivery zone rather than chase orders we cannot serve hot. Beyond that, it is not our chilla any more.

Crispness, tested at the counter

Every recipe goes through a timed box test before it reaches the menu. We score the edge at three intervals — anything that drops below the line gets a packaging change, not a menu listing.

Good to know

Questions we expect.

Is chilla gluten-free?
The besan and dal chillas are — gram flour and lentils contain no gluten. The oats chilla and anything with sooji are not. We cook on a shared tawa, so if you are coeliac rather than avoiding gluten by preference, please tell us at the counter and we will be straight with you about cross-contact.
Do you use maida anywhere?
No. Not in the batter, not as a binder, not in the rolls. The chilla itself is the wrap. If a chilla needs body, it gets besan or a little fine sooji, both of which are on the menu card.
Can I get it without onion and garlic?
Yes, on any chilla. Ask for Jain and we prep it on a cleaned section of the tawa with a separate spatula. The stuffing swaps to paneer, capsicum and corn.
Is the kitchen vegetarian?
Everything is vegetarian except one item — the Egg Chilla. It is cooked on its own section of the tawa, with its own spatula and its own prep tray, and it is labelled on the box.
Will it still be crisp when it arrives?
At 15 and 30 minutes, yes — that is what the vented box and the separate chutney pot are for. Past 45 minutes the edge softens, which is why we cap delivery at a 3–5 km radius instead of taking every order on the map.
Do you do office breakfasts and bulk orders?
Yes. Give us the head count and a time slot the evening before and we will batch the prep, not the cooking — chillas still hit the tawa on site so they arrive hot. Use the form below.
